# BIFF Cheatsheet

Use this while the draft is open. One page of decisions.

## Reply or stay silent

| If you see this | Do this | Because |
|---|---|---|
| Only the two of you, no real issue, pure insult, mind won't change, or you already answered | Do not reply | Another paragraph feeds the next attack |
| Others were copied, it's public, or silence will be told as agreement | Reply the same day, same people, same channel | HCPs treat silence as consent, and emotion makes the story feel true |
| You opened a request (a schedule change, a document) | Close it in one short note, even if the rest is abuse | Leaving a request hanging looks like you are still negotiating |
| They claim unnamed allies but copied no one | Reply only to them | You would be the person who made it big |
| You cannot control their channel (a show that mocked you) | Write your own short public note to that community | You need the same audience without handing them the edit |

## Before the first sentence

Pick one goal: **manage**, **reduce**, or **phase out**.

- Manage: next behavior, policy, shared task.
- Reduce: no to this specific request. No essay about the relationship.
- Phase out: slow limits. No sudden "we're done" speech.

## The paragraph

1. Friendly open: "Thanks for your email" or "Okay, [name]."
2. One or two neutral facts. No character defense.
3. Firm close: the request is withdrawn, the document is attached, or two choices plus a date.
4. Stop.

Target: **2–5 sentences**. A public note may run longer if it must correct a specific false claim. Still one screen.

## Edit pass — delete these

| Cut it | Unless |
|---|---|
| Admonishment ("you should know better") | Never in a BIFF |
| Advice they did not ask for | They asked, in this message, for that advice |
| "I'm sorry I did X to you" | It is only a social nicety ("sorry I'm late") |
| Point-by-point rebuttal of insults | A group saw it — then one sentence: "Almost all of that is inaccurate." Invite private questions |
| The nickname, the diagnosis, "look in the mirror" | Never |
| The threat they made, restated by you | Handle safety offline. In the note, remove their reason to show up |
| A second offer they already refused | Once is enough. Repeating it sounds like a criticism |
| "Should" | Try "could," or skip the suggestion |

## Firm lines you can adapt

- "I've said all I'm going to say on this subject."
- "If I don't have [fact] by [date], I will [specific next step]. I hope that won't be necessary."
- "You have two choices. The easy way is [calm action]. The hard way is [consequence you will actually carry out]."
- "I respect your privacy. If I don't hear from you now and then, I worry and feel like checking on you." (consequence about *your* action, not an order)

## Who else is on the thread

| Audience | Move |
|---|---|
| Potential negative advocates | Short facts, no attack on the sender, invite them to ask you. Most will drop out |
| Your own boss who blamed you upward | Correct the work facts to the person who received the blame. Do not call the boss a liar in that note |
| A board or meeting | Thank them, say you will look it up, say where the result will be posted, call the next speaker |
| After termination | Glad they're interviewing. Attach the practical item. No lecture. Tell security separately if they threatened to visit |

## Smells

- You feel a twenty-minute high while typing. That is counter-Blamespeak. Walk, then cut by half.
- The draft explains why they are wrong about you. Delete that half.
- You are about to apologize to calm them. They will store it.
- Sarcasm in quotes ("the woman you love"). It will be read as contempt.
- You named them in a public reply. Talk about the concern and your decision instead.

## Coaching order

Brief? Informative? Friendly? Firm? Advice? Admonishment? Apology? How will they respond? What would you change? Only then: "Want my thoughts?" The writer decides.
