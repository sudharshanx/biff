# Patterns

## Write the BIFF

**When to use**: You decided a reply is worth sending.
**How**: One paragraph. Friendly open, neutral facts, firm close. 2–5 sentences. No 3 A's. No point-by-point defense. Have a second person cut it.
**Trade-offs**: You will not feel the satisfaction of answering the insult. You may solve the actual problem and you will hand them fewer hooks.

## Choose silence

**When to use**: Private thread, no live issue, opinion-only attack, or you already answered.
**How**: Do not send. Do not explain the silence.
**Trade-offs**: They may tell others you agreed. If that risk is real, this pattern is the wrong one. Use a fast factual reply instead.

## Match the audience

**When to use**: The attack was copied or made public.
**How**: Reply to those same people, in that format, quickly. One correction of the record, not a trial. "Almost all of that is inaccurate. Ask me if you need a fact."
**Trade-offs**: You re-enter a public story. Waiting lets the emotion settle in as fact.

## Indirect confrontation

**When to use**: You need different behavior from someone who melts down when criticized.
**How**: Desired future behavior + policy or schedule reason. Past mentioned once, if you must, then the rest is future.
**Trade-offs**: Looks soft to bystanders who wanted a scolding. Works more often than the scolding.

## Inform negative advocates

**When to use**: They forwarded the attack to neighbors, family, or a board.
**How**: Short note. Here is the proposal already on the table. Ask us if you have a question. No character attack.
**Trade-offs**: You engage people you wish were not involved. You also stop them absorbing only one side.

## Two choices

**When to use**: A limit is required and a bare order will escalate.
**How**: Easy way and hard way. You state what you will do. Short clock. If they cannot choose, apply the hard way.
**Trade-offs**: You must be willing to carry out the hard way. Empty consequences teach the opposite lesson.

## Consequence, not threat

**When to use**: A rule will be enforced (code, HR, security, parenting order).
**How**: "If this isn't done by [date], [the rule] requires [next step]. I hope we can avoid that." Us-against-the-problem, not us-against-you.
**Trade-offs**: Hostile tone feels stronger and backfires. Calm tone is easier to ignore if you never follow through.

## Do not mention the threat

**When to use**: They threaten to expose you, sue, visit, or melt down.
**How**: Protect offline (security, counsel, police). In the writing, satisfy the practical request and do not describe the scene you fear.
**Trade-offs**: You may look like you missed the threat. You also avoid handing them a picture to act out.

## Phase out a relationship

**When to use**: You want less contact with a friend or relative who escalates when rejected.
**How**: Refuse this visit, this loan, this move-in. Give a narrow alternative if you truly will help. Do not write the history of the friendship.
**Trade-offs**: Slower than a breakup speech. Less likely to produce a pursuit spike.

## Stay on the question

**When to use**: They answered your request with a new attack and a new demand.
**How**: One clause on their real concern, then back to your ask. Offer the same flexibility you want. Put the reasons in writing in case you need the record. If they continue: one closing line, then stop.
**Trade-offs**: You will not correct every slur. The record will show a reasonable request.

## Distract onto a neutral topic

**When to use**: Verbal follow-up with someone who is spinning up, and you know a safe subject.
**How**: After the factual BIFF, move to a neutral interest of theirs. Do not use this to mock them.
**Trade-offs**: Does not always work. Know the relationship before you try it with a boss.

## One handler

**When to use**: An organization keeps getting the same complainant through different staff.
**How**: Assign one person. Rotate them off after about 6–12 months so they do not burn out.
**Trade-offs**: That person absorbs the heat. Splitting the heat across staff is worse, because limits get undone.

## Coach with 10 questions

**When to use**: Someone asks you to review a draft.
**How**: They read it aloud. You ask Brief, Informative, Friendly, Firm, Advice, Admonishment, Apology, predicted response, their own edits, then "want my thoughts?" Suggestions are tentative and plural. They choose.
**Trade-offs**: Slower than rewriting it for them. They learn to do the next one alone.

## Public correction

**When to use**: A false, specific accusation is circulating (fraud, a viral incident, a news claim).
**How**: Brief, warm toward the audience, factual, future-focused. Do not use the accuser's name. Do not trade insults. Specific claims get answered. Vague campaign rhetoric can be ignored.
**Trade-offs**: You feed a news cycle if you answer a vague insult. You lose the record if you ignore a specific one.
